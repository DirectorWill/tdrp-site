DEL RIO, Texas — Val Verde Regional Medical Center’s da Vinci 5 surgical system has supported 100 procedures since the hospital’s robotic surgery program launched June 1, 2026, the hospital said in a [news release](https://www.vvrmc.org/news/releases/vvrmc-s-da-vinci-5-surgical-system-reaches-100-procedures-since-launch/) dated Oct. 27 and posted Oct. 28.

The program brings together physicians and care teams across multiple specialties, according to the release. Participating physicians named by VVRMC include Dr. Wyn Nguyen, Dr. Shawn Nichols, Dr. Antonio Castañeda and Dr. Ashley Lopez.

The da Vinci 5 is designed to help surgeons with visualization, precision and control during minimally invasive procedures, the hospital said. Robotic-assisted surgery is performed by a surgeon who guides the instruments; the system does not operate on its own, according to the release.

“Reaching 100 procedures since launching the program is an important milestone for our hospital and our community,” Jorge Jurado, CEO of VVRMC, said in the release. “Our goal is to expand access to advanced surgical options close to home. This progress reflects the skill and commitment of our surgical teams and the trust patients place in VVRMC.”

VVRMC said the milestone also reflects work by nurses, surgical technologists, anesthesia professionals and other care team members. Whether robotic-assisted surgery is appropriate depends on each patient’s condition and treatment plan, and patients should talk with their health care provider, the hospital said.
